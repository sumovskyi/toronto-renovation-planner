# Renovation ideas: options that fit the space and the budget

Idea patterns, constraints and common mistakes are in `references/ideas.md`. Budget numbers come from `renovation-cost-estimator`.

## Steps
1. **Understand the space and the use**: room, size, ceiling height (basements), light/windows, what stays (stairs, drains, furnace), who uses it and for what, budget feel.
2. **Offer 3 ideas at different budget levels** (for example: open family room · family room + bathroom · guest suite). For each: what it is, why it fits their use, **what it depends on** (drain location, ceiling height, egress, ventilation, structure), rough budget range with source, and one tip that keeps the cost down.
3. **Cheapest-way and value questions**: answer directly (keep the layout open, keep wet areas near existing drains, don't move the stairs or the kitchen sink unless it matters, standard sizes, phase finishes). Value: in Toronto, extra legal living space, a second bathroom and functional kitchens generally matter more than luxury finishes. Avoid resale-return percentages from U.S. data.
4. **Mistakes to avoid** for this project (from the reference).
5. **Next step**: turn the chosen idea into a budget (`renovation-cost-estimator`) or a brief for contractors (`renovation-brief`).

## Rules
- Practical over trendy. Name the constraint before the idea gets expensive (e.g. a basement bathroom far from the drain needs a pump; a bedroom needs an egress window).
- No health claims (e.g. about lighting or air) and no guaranteed resale value.
- Use descriptions, not copyrighted images. Suggest searching public project galleries for photos.
