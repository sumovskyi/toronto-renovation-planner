# Planning reference (Ontario; checked 2026-09-29, general guidance)

## Usually needs a building permit (confirm with the city)
Structural changes (removing or altering load-bearing walls, beams, new openings) · new or moved plumbing (adding a bathroom or kitchen) · HVAC changes (new ductwork, furnace/relocation) · finishing a basement in many cities · creating a second unit · new or enlarged window/door openings (e.g. egress) · underpinning or lowering a floor · additions, decks above a set height, garages · backwater valve installation (Toronto) · demolition of structure.
Electrical work needs an Electrical Safety Authority (ESA) permit/notification through a licensed electrical contractor.

## Usually doesn't need a building permit
Painting, flooring, cabinets replaced in the same place, countertops, trim, like-for-like fixture replacement without moving plumbing. Cities differ: always confirm.

## Stage timelines (typical ranges; vary with scope, approvals and supply)
| Stage | Typical range | What makes it longer |
|---|---|---|
| Design and drawings | 2–8 weeks | Layout changes, engineering, many revisions |
| Permit review | a few weeks for simple interior work to several months for additions / variances | Incomplete drawings, zoning variance (Committee of Adjustment) |
| Long-lead orders | 2–10 weeks (cabinets, windows, custom items) | Custom sizes, imports |
| Construction: basement finish | ~4–10 weeks | Bathroom, moisture work, legal-suite requirements |
| Construction: kitchen | ~4–10 weeks without a working kitchen | Layout/structural changes |
| Construction: bathroom | ~2–5 weeks | Moving plumbing, custom glass |
| Construction: addition | ~3–6+ months | Foundation, weather, roof tie-in |
| Construction: full-home | ~3–9+ months | Structure, systems, occupied home |
| Inspections | Book per stage (framing, insulation, plumbing, final) | Failed inspection, rework |
These are planning ranges, not promises. Use the contractor's written schedule.

## Toronto lot permissions (secondary summary; verify on toronto.ca)
- Up to four units (multiplex) as-of-right in residential zones citywide since May 2023; sixplexes as-of-right in Toronto & East York south of Eglinton and in Ward 23 (2025).
- Garden suites and laneway suites permitted citywide (since 2022), in addition to the main building's units.
- No minimum parking citywide. Development charges reportedly waived for multiplex projects up to six units.
Source (secondary): https://www.plexora.ca/blog/toronto-multiplex-zoning-guide-2026 — confirm with the City's current planning pages.
- Anything outside the zoning by-law (setbacks, height, coverage) needs a minor variance from the Committee of Adjustment, which adds months.

## Living through it
- Kitchen: set up a temporary kitchen (fridge, microwave, kettle, induction plate) in another room; plan meals; protect the path from the door.
- One-bathroom homes: ask for the sequence that keeps a working toilet as long as possible; consider a gym/family membership for showers or a short stay elsewhere for the critical days.
- Full-home/occupied: phase by floor, seal dust zones, agree on quiet hours and a weekly update.
- Basement: HVAC returns sealed during drywall sanding; plan storage for moved items.

## Contingency
10–20% of construction cost; older homes toward the high end.
