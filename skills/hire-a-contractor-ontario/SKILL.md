# Hire a contractor: a vetting method that protects the homeowner

## Steps

1. **Shortlist 3 contractors** that actually do this kind of project in the homeowner's area (portfolio with similar projects, recent reviews that mention timing and budget, a real address).
2. **Run the official checks** for each:
   - **Toronto business licence**: businesses that renovate or advertise renovation services in Toronto need a City of Toronto Building Renovator licence. Check it with the City of Toronto Business Licence Lookup (secure.toronto.ca/LicenceStatus) or by calling 311. Other cities: ask the city.
   - **WSIB clearance certificate**: free online lookup at https://clearances.wsib.ca/Clearances/eclearance/start?lang=en. A valid clearance helps protect the homeowner from the contractor's unpaid WSIB premiums.
   - **Liability insurance certificate**: ask for a copy showing the coverage amount and expiry date.
   - **HST registration**: the number should be on the quote.
3. **Read reviews like an inspector**: look for recent reviews that mention the same project type, schedule, budget and how problems were handled after completion, and at how the owner answers negative reviews.
4. **Get comparable quotes**: send all three the same scope. Use `contractor-quote-checker` to compare line by line.
5. **Before signing**: a written contract with scope, materials, schedule, payment schedule tied to milestones, a deposit of about 10% or less, warranty terms in writing, a change-order process, who pulls permits, and cleanup. Ontario rights: 10-day cooling-off if signed at home; the final price can't exceed the estimate by more than 10% without agreement (https://www.ontario.ca/page/your-rights-when-starting-home-renovations-or-repairs).
6. **Red flags**: cash-only or cash discounts, large deposits, "no permit needed" for structural, plumbing, egress or suite work, no written scope, pressure to sign today, no address or licence.

## Questions to ask every contractor (tailor to the project)

Who will be my one contact, and how often will I get updates? · Who pulls the permits, and in whose name? · What happens if you find water, mould or old wiring? · How do you price changes, and do I approve them in writing first? · What does your warranty cover, for how long, and how do I make a claim? · Can I see or call a client with a similar project from the last 12 months?
