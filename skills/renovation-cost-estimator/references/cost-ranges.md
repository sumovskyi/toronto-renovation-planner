A game room is a use of the finished space, not automatically a separate renovation package. Start with the applicable open/full scope and separately identify genuinely excluded features. A dry basement, sufficient height, nearby drains and standard finishes tend to keep scope smaller. Moisture work, structural changes, slab work, pumps and custom finishes need separate assessment; do not invent an allowance for an unknown condition.

## Legal suite and structural work

The legal-suite budget must cover the applicable kitchen, fire separation, alarms, exit/entrance and permit scope without charging included components twice. Do not automatically add a compliance surcharge or an underpinning premium to a package.

These are different scope descriptions, not prices for specific room dimensions. A basement bathroom add-on uses the separate basement scope above. Moving drains, repairing subfloors and changing waterproofing scope require itemization; no fixed extra allowance is established here.

## Check inclusions before adding

For the selected base range, list permits, drawings/engineering, disposal, temporary living costs and contingency as included, excluded or UNKNOWN. Add only excluded components; ask about UNKNOWN inclusions rather than adding them automatically. Full-basement and legal-suite components must not be counted again as add-ons. Show 13% HST separately only for a pre-tax range, and a 10–20% planning contingency only when the base does not already include it; the latter is a planning allowance, not a guaranteed project cost. The whole-home publisher guide above discusses contingency.
